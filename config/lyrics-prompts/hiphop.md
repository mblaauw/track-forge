Hip-hop lyrics must work as performed music, not merely as written poetry.

Write every line for a clear rhythmic pocket. Maintain a consistent cadence within each section, while allowing deliberate variations at transitions, punchlines, and section endings. Lines should be naturally rappable without awkward compression, filler words, or unnatural word order.

Treat each requested lyric line as one musical bar unless the section specification explicitly states otherwise. Respect the requested line count exactly.

For verses:

- Develop the subject rather than repeating the brief in different words.
- Use concrete details, actions, images, observations, and memorable phrasing.
- Maintain a coherent perspective and progression from the first line to the last.
- Use internal rhyme, multisyllabic rhyme, consonance, and controlled end-rhyme where appropriate for the preset.
- Prefer rhyme chains that continue across multiple bars rather than isolated rhyming couplets.
- Vary sentence length and placement of pauses while preserving the section's rhythmic pocket.
- Build toward the final bars; do not let the verse become a collection of unrelated punchlines.

For hooks:

- Reduce the song's central idea to one immediate, quotable phrase.
- Use simpler language, stronger repetition, and clearer vowel sounds than in the verses.
- Make the hook noticeably different from the verse in cadence and phrasing.
- Repeat key words intentionally, with small variations that develop the meaning.
- Avoid summarizing the entire verse or introducing unrelated information.

Match the writing to the specified delivery:

- laid-back: spacious phrasing, fewer syllables, controlled confidence;
- rhythmic: strong stress patterns, syncopation, and internal movement;
- melodic: open vowels, sustained words, and uncomplicated syntax;
- hype: short attacks, repetition, call-and-response, and strong downbeat words;
- intense: compressed phrasing, sharper consonants, escalating imagery, and minimal conversational filler.

Parenthetical vocal ad-libs such as "(yeah)" or "(look)" are allowed when the section requests ad-libs. Use them sparingly and place them where a performer could naturally deliver them. Do not write production instructions, performance notes, or descriptions as lyric lines.

Avoid generic rap filler and overused imagery unless the brief specifically requires it. Do not rely on vague references to crowns, thrones, flames, demons, shadows, haters, grinding, being unstoppable, or reaching the top. Prefer language that could only belong to this song and narrator.

Do not force every line to rhyme. Meaning, cadence, and natural delivery take priority over obvious rhyme. Never distort grammar solely to complete a rhyme.

Write in the requested perspective and tense. The narrator may address another person, quote dialogue, or briefly shift sentence structure when it remains consistent with that perspective.
