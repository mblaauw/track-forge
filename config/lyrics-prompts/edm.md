EDM lyrics are written for repetition, emotional lift, and physical delivery. They should feel natural when sung over a large arrangement and immediate enough for a crowd to remember after one listen.

Prioritize hook economy over lyrical density. The central hook should be built around one short, title-worthy line supported by no more than a few complementary phrases. Hooks should use clear language, strong vowel sounds, deliberate repetition, and an immediately understandable emotion.

Match the lyrical complexity to the selected EDM preset. Dance-pop may use a simple narrative and more complete verses, while festival-oriented styles should favor shorter phrases, repetition, and broader emotional statements.

For verses:

- Establish or advance one simple emotional situation.
- Use approximately one vocal phrase for every one to two musical bars unless the section explicitly requests another density.
- Keep sentences short, melodic, and easy to sing.
- Use selective concrete imagery, but do not overload the verse with names, explanations, events, or intricate details.
- Let each verse move the song toward the hook.
- Verse 2 must advance, complicate, or resolve what Verse 1 established rather than restarting the same idea.

For pre-choruses and builds:

- Reduce lyrical density as the arrangement rises.
- Use short lines, repeated syntax, and increasingly direct language.
- Create anticipation without revealing a completely new subject.
- The final line should naturally lead into the chorus or drop.

For choruses and vocal hooks:

- Center the section on one quotable title phrase.
- Use one to four short core phrases.
- Repeat the title phrase substantially unchanged.
- Use minor variations only when they increase intensity or complete the emotional progression.
- Keep the hook simpler, shorter, and more rhythmically predictable than the verses.
- Prefer open vowels and words that can be sustained or shouted clearly.
- Avoid introducing new plot details in the chorus.

For breakdowns:

- Use zero to four sparse vocal phrases.
- Restate the song's central emotion in a more vulnerable, intimate, or suspended form.
- Leave space between phrases for the instrumental arrangement.

For drops and instrumental builds:

- Return no lyrics unless the section explicitly requests a vocal chop, chant, pickup, or repeated phrase.
- When vocals are requested, use one very short phrase rather than a complete verse.

A lyric line represents a performed vocal phrase, not necessarily one musical bar. Do not add extra lines merely to match the section's bar count. Follow the requested lyric-line target separately from the musical duration.

Use natural rhyme sparingly. Favor melodic phrasing, repetition, vowel consistency, and rhythmic clarity over dense end rhyme, multisyllabic rhyme, or rapid syllable runs.

Avoid generic collections of EDM imagery. Do not automatically combine references to neon lights, city nights, fire, stars, being alive, losing control, touching the sky, and dancing until sunrise. Choose a small number of images that belong specifically to the supplied brief.

When a storytelling arc is requested, keep the story simple enough to understand during a first listen. Establish the situation in Verse 1, introduce a decision or emotional turn before or during Verse 2, and let the final chorus express the resulting emotional release.

Match the supplied mood, energy, tempo feel, perspective, and vocal delivery. Higher energy should increase immediacy and repetition, not necessarily lyrical density.

Write only sung lyric lines inside `lines`. Do not include performance directions, arrangement descriptions, harmony labels, section headers, bar numbers, or production instructions as lyrics.
